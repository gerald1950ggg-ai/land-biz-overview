County Selection Rubric

Land Profit Generator Method — 6-Step Screen
1 Pick State — Disclosure Check
GO Full disclosure state (sale prices public)
CAUTION Non-disclosure — harder comps, skip if starting out
Non-disclosure: TX, ID, UT, AK, MS, ND, KS, LA, MO, MT, NM, WY
2 Target Metro — Population ≥ 750K
Find the nearest metro (MSA) to your target county.
GO Metro pop ≥ 750K (ideally 1M+)
NO-GO Metro pop < 750K
Source: Google "[city] metro area population" or Census MSA tables
3 Donut Distance — 30 to 100 Miles
Measure driving distance from metro center to county seat.
TOO CLOSE < 30 mi — land too expensive
SWEET SPOT 30–100 mi — growth expansion zone
TOO FAR > 100 mi — buyer pool thins out
Use Google Maps "Measure distance" · Metros expand outward — be ahead of the growth, not in it
4 Infrastructure & Topography
Google Maps satellite view — scan for red flags:
AVOID No road grid (landlocked), desert, aggressive slopes
GO County roads on grid, usable terrain, low flood risk
Ask: "Is there an obvious reason people wouldn't want to live here?" · Check FEMA flood map (msc.fema.gov)
5 Sold-to-Active Ratio (Zillow) — 20% to 130%
Zillow → Lots/Land only → your acreage range (1–5 ac).
Active: "For Sale" → any days → record total. Sold: "Sold" → 12 months → record total.
Formula: Sold ÷ Active × 100 = STR%
DEAD STR < 20% — market too slow, land sits
IDEAL STR 20–130% — competitive balance
HOT STR > 130% — seller's market, may overpay
LOW SAMPLE Sold count < 50 — insufficient data, skip
Cross-check on Redfin — if within 25%, average them. If not, investigate.
6 Market Values — $5K to $300K Sweet Spot
Same Zillow filter — sort by price, scan the distribution.
GO 50%+ of listings priced $5K–$300K
TOO CHEAP Most < $5K — profit eaten by fixed costs
TOO PRICEY Most > $300K — complex deals, slow turns
Best sweet spot: $10K–$100K for rapid acquisition & sale
