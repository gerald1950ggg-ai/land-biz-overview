## 1. LLC vs. S-Corp for Land Flipping

### The Short Answer
**Start as an LLC. Elect S-Corp tax treatment once you're consistently generating $50K+ in annual net flip profit.**

### Why It Matters for Flippers Specifically
Land flipping income is **active dealer income** (IRS dealer status, IRC §1221). Unlike passive rental income—which is already exempt from self-employment tax—flip profits flow to you as ordinary income subject to:
- Federal income tax at your marginal rate
- **Self-employment tax: 15.3%** on the first ~$176,100, then 2.9% above that (2024 rates)

This is different from buy-and-hold real estate, where passive rental income is SE-tax-exempt. **For flippers, the SE tax savings from an S-Corp election are real.**

### LLC (Default — Partnership Taxation)

| | LLC (Multi-Member) |
|---|---|
| **Tax filing** | Form 1065 + K-1s to each member |
| **SE tax on profits** | 15.3% on 100% of your share |
| **Compliance cost** | Low — partnership return, no payroll |
| **Flexibility** | High — easy allocations, flexible governance |
| **Asset protection** | Yes — members not personally liable for business debts |

**Example:** $100K net profit, split 50/50 = $50K each. Each partner pays SE tax on the full $50K = ~$7,650 each. Total SE tax: **~$15,300**.

### LLC Taxed as S-Corp

| | S-Corp (via Form 2553 election) |
|---|---|
| **Tax filing** | Form 1120-S + W-2s + payroll |
| **SE tax on profits** | Only on "reasonable salary" portion |
| **Compliance cost** | ~$2,000–$5,000/yr (payroll service + CPA) |
| **Flexibility** | Moderate — compensation scrutiny, no special allocations |
| **Asset protection** | Same as LLC |

**Example:** $100K profit, $40K each. Each takes $20K salary + $20K distribution. SE tax applies only to the $20K salary portion = ~$3,060 each. Total SE tax: **~$6,120**. **Annual savings: ~$9,180.**

### The Break-Even Analysis
The S-Corp election makes financial sense when SE tax savings exceed added compliance costs (~$3,000/yr):

- At $20K/year net profit per partner: savings ~$1,500 — **S-Corp not worth it yet**
- At $50K/year net profit per partner: savings ~$6,800 — **borderline, consider it**
- At $100K+/year net profit per partner: savings $12,000+ — **S-Corp clearly wins**

### Recommendation for Marcus & Chris
**Year 1:** Form the LLC under default partnership tax treatment (Form 1065). Low overhead, easy to start, appropriate for initial deal flow.  
**Year 2+:** Once consistently generating $80K+/year combined profit, file **Form 2553** to elect S-Corp status. Have a CPA run the exact numbers first.

> ⚠️ **Colorado has no state-level franchise tax on LLCs.** The S-Corp election doesn't add state tax complexity beyond the federal level here.

## 2. Colorado LLC Formation Steps

### Step 1: Choose a Name
- Must include "LLC," "L.L.C.," "Limited Liability Company," or an approved abbreviation
- Must be **distinguishable** from existing Colorado business names
- **Check availability:** [Colorado SOS Business Search](https://www.sos.state.co.us/biz/BusinessEntityCriteriaExt.do)
- Optional: Reserve the name for 120 days by filing a **Statement of Reservation of Name** ($25)

### Step 2: Choose a Registered Agent *(see Section 8 for full details)*
You must designate a registered agent before filing Articles of Organization.

### Step 3: File Articles of Organization Online
- **Where:** [Colorado Secretary of State Online Filing](https://www.sos.state.co.us/biz/FileDocumentGetStarted.do)
- **What to enter:**
  - LLC name
  - Principal office address (Marcus's Colorado address)
  - Registered agent name and Colorado address
  - Whether LLC is manager-managed or member-managed
  - Name(s) of person(s) forming the LLC (can be one person)
- **Cost:** $50 (online only — no paper option for LLCs)
- **Timeline:** **Immediate** upon payment processing

### Step 4: Draft Operating Agreement *(see Section 5)*
Not required by Colorado law but **essential** for a 50/50 two-member LLC. Sign and date it; keep it with your business records. You do **not** file it with the state.

### Step 5: Get an EIN *(see Section 6)*

### Step 6: Open a Business Bank Account *(see Section 7)*

### Step 7: Annual Periodic Report
- **Due:** During the three-month window starting the anniversary month of formation
- **Cost:** $25/year (online filing)
- **Example:** Formed July 14 → report due July 1 – September 30 each year
- **Late penalty:** $50
- **File at:** [Colorado SOS Online Portal](https://www.sos.state.co.us/biz/)

## 5. Operating Agreement Essentials for 50/50 Two-Member LLCs

Colorado doesn't require a written operating agreement, but a 50/50 LLC **without one is a lawsuit waiting to happen**. Two equal members with no tiebreaker = potential paralysis on every major decision.

### Must-Have Provisions

**1. Ownership & Capital**
- Each member owns 50% of the LLC
- Initial capital contributions (document what each person is putting in — cash, property, sweat equity)
- Capital account maintenance rules
- Who is required to contribute more if the LLC needs cash (capital call procedures)

**2. Management Structure**
Choose one:
- **Member-managed** (recommended for 2-person LLC): Both Marcus and Chris manage day-to-day
- **Manager-managed**: One person is designated manager
- For 50/50, member-managed keeps it simple and equal

**3. Voting & Decision-Making**
- What decisions require unanimous consent (major — selling real estate, taking on debt, adding members)
- What can one member act on alone (routine operational decisions under a dollar threshold)
- Recommended: Set a threshold (e.g., any decision involving >$5,000 requires both to agree)

**4. Profit & Loss Allocation**
- 50% to each member
- Specify timing of distributions (quarterly? when a property closes?)
- Can profits be retained in the LLC vs. distributed?

**5. Deadlock Resolution — CRITICAL**
Without this, the LLC can become ungovernable if Marcus and Chris disagree.

**Recommended three-step escalation:**
1. **Mandatory mediation** — 30 days to attempt resolution with a neutral third party
2. **Binding arbitration** — if mediation fails, neutral arbitrator decides
3. **Shotgun/Buy-Sell clause** — if business-ending deadlock: either member names a price for the full 50% interest; the other must **buy** or **sell** at that price within 30–60 days

> **Why the shotgun clause works:** Because either party could end up on the buying or selling side, there's strong incentive to name a fair price. Low-ball offers can get you bought out cheaply.

**6. Transfer Restrictions**
- Neither member can sell or transfer their interest without the other's written consent
- Right of First Refusal: if one member wants to sell, the other has the right to buy at the same price before any third party
- What happens if one member dies, becomes disabled, or goes bankrupt?

**7. Buy-Sell Triggers**
Document what events trigger a forced buyout:
- Death of a member (life insurance-funded buyout is cleanest)
- Permanent disability
- Personal bankruptcy
- Voluntary withdrawal
- Conviction of a felony

**8. Dissolution**
- Process for winding down (vote required, liquidation priority)
- What happens to real estate held by the LLC at dissolution?

### Resources for Drafting
- **DIY:** Northwestern Mutual's free templates, Law Depot ($50–100), LegalZoom ($99–199)
- **Recommended:** Have a Colorado business attorney review it — $500–$1,500 for a basic agreement is cheap insurance for a 50/50 partnership
- **Search for Colorado-specific templates:** Many standard templates are state-agnostic; a CO attorney ensures compliance with Colorado's LLC Act (CRS Title 7, Article 80)

## 6. EIN Registration (IRS)

An Employer Identification Number (EIN) is required for:
- Business banking (every bank will ask for it)
- Filing your LLC's partnership tax return (Form 1065)
- Paying any employees

### How to Get One
1. Go to [IRS EIN Online Application](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online)
2. Select: **Limited Liability Company**
3. Enter number of members: **2**
4. Complete the application (takes 10–15 minutes)
5. You receive the EIN **immediately** at the end of the session
6. Save the EIN confirmation letter (form SS-4) — banks will ask for it

**Cost:** Free  
**Who can apply:** Must be principal officer, general partner, grantor, owner, or trustee

## 7. Business Bank Account Requirements

Opening a business account for your LLC typically requires:

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Articles of Organization | Your filed copy from Colorado SOS (the confirmation email/PDF works) |
| EIN confirmation | IRS confirmation letter or SS-4 |
| Operating Agreement | Most banks want to see it — another reason to have one |
| Government-issued ID | Both members may need to appear or provide ID |
| Initial deposit | Varies by bank — can be as low as $0–$100 |

### Recommended Banks for Small LLCs
- **Mercury** (mercury.com) — Online bank, great for small LLCs, no monthly fees, easy account opening, good for ACH payments to sellers/title companies
- **Chase Business Complete** — Branch access, widely accepted, $15/mo fee (waivable)
- **Bank of America** — Good if you want branch banking
- **Relay** — Another solid online option for two-owner businesses with sub-accounts

### Why This Matters
Commingling personal and business funds is the #1 way to lose your LLC's liability protection (piercing the corporate veil). Open the account before doing any deals.

## 8. Registered Agent

### Requirements (Updated July 2025)
Colorado updated its registered agent requirements via HB24-1137, effective July 1, 2025:

**If using an individual as registered agent (e.g., Marcus):**
- Must be 18+
- Must have a Colorado driver's license or state ID
- Must have a primary residence **or** usual place of business in Colorado
- Must be physically available to receive documents during normal business hours

**If using a professional service:**
- Must be in good standing with the Colorado SOS
- Must maintain a physical Colorado address (no P.O. boxes)

## 9. Colorado-Specific Land/Real Estate LLC Considerations

### Colorado Is a Full Disclosure State
Colorado **does** publicly record real estate sale prices (CRS § 39-13-101). This means:
- Comparable sales data is accessible through county assessor records
- Zillow/Redfin/Realtor.com data is generally reliable in Colorado
- No PropStream AVM workarounds needed for in-state deals

### Documentary Fee
Colorado charges a documentary fee on property transfers: **$0.01 per $100 of sale price** (or $1 per $10,000). On a $20,000 land sale, that's $2. Not significant.

### No State Transfer Tax
Colorado has no state-level real estate transfer tax. Only the documentary fee above.

### Annual Periodic Report Timing
The report is due during a **5-month window**: two months before through two months after the anniversary of formation. Don't miss it — late penalty is $50, and extended failure can result in administrative dissolution.

### Land-Specific Note
If the LLC holds land in Colorado and you later sell to a developer or via owner financing, the operating agreement should address how title is held, who signs deeds, and the authority threshold for one member to act alone vs. requiring both signatures.

## 10. Brand Name Research Approach

Before committing to an LLC name:

### Step 1: Colorado SOS Search
- Go to: [https://www.sos.state.co.us/biz/BusinessEntityCriteriaExt.do](https://www.sos.state.co.us/biz/BusinessEntityCriteriaExt.do)
- Search the exact name **and** similar names
- Colorado requires "distinguishable" — not just "different" — so "Colorado Land Pros LLC" and "CO Land Pro LLC" may conflict

### Step 2: Federal Trademark Search
- Go to: [USPTO TESS](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/)
- Search for the name in the "Live" and "Dead" databases
- Search for the name as both **standard characters** and **design marks**
- If someone has a live trademark on the same/similar name in a related class (real estate), you're at risk

### Step 3: Domain Check
- [Namecheap.com](https://www.namecheap.com) or Google Domains — check if the .com is available
- Also check .co, .io if .com is taken

### Step 4: Social Handles
- [Namecheckr.com](https://www.namecheckr.com) — checks availability across Instagram, Twitter/X, Facebook, etc.

### Name Format Guidance
For a land flipping LLC, the name doesn't need to advertise the business — **use something that works on a deed and a contract**. Keep it short. Examples: "Skyline Land LLC," "Ridgeline Acquisitions LLC," "Summit Land Partners LLC."
